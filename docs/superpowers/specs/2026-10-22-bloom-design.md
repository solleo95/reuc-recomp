# Bloom replacement (design)

Date: 2026-10-22. Branch: `feature/step-audit` (HDR output work is uncommitted there; this builds on `renderer/hdr_pass.*`, `renderer/shaders/hdr.slang`).

## Intent

The game's own bloom is a half-resolution (logically 320x180) blur chain whose result is added by one draw (callsite `0x800421B8`, `gx::bloom_composite`, `renderer/post_effects.h`). At 1440p/4K it looks blocky and clipped, and in HDR it cannot exceed white. The owner wants it replaced completely by a good-looking multi-level bloom that works in SDR and in HDR.

The owner chose "variant 5" from a comparison sheet (`build/bloom_variants.png`, generated by a throwaway script, not part of the repo): a physically-motivated bloom, no threshold, Karis-filtered first downsample, six pyramid levels, soft tent upsample, low strength. In SDR it reads as a soft, natural glow around lamps, windows and speculars; in HDR the glow and the light sources go above paper white towards the configured peak.

## Goals

- A new bloom replaces the game's: the game's composite draw is always skipped while the new bloom is enabled (it is skipped today when `graphics.bloom` is off).
- Looks right in SDR and in HDR output (`graphics.hdr` on and active).
- Two player settings: an on/off checkbox and a strength slider (0..200 %, default 100 %). Both in the launcher (Graphics page) and the in-game menu (Insert), live (no restart), saved in `config.json`, CLI override.
- Works with every upscaler (off, SMAA, DLSS, FSR) and with frame generation; the HUD does not blur.
- Deterministic runs (`--deterministic`, demos, `det_check`) stay bit-identical run to run (same input, same pass, no temporal state).

## Non-goals

- No switch back to the original game bloom (owner: the new one fully replaces it). Off means no bloom at all.
- No lens flares, dirt mask, anamorphic streaks, no per-light colour grading, no temporal accumulation.
- No change to the HDR peak/paper-white model. No new HDR metadata. Native game HUD brightness stays out of scope.
- Skipping the guest's now-useless bloom blur work (the half-resolution copies and blurs the game still does) is a possible later saving, not part of this.

## Anti-goals (failure even if it technically works)

- Flickering or "fireflies" on single bright pixels (that is why the first downsample uses the Karis average).
- A bloom that washes the whole frame grey, or lifts blacks (no threshold means every level adds energy, so the strength is tuned to be energy-small: default about 15 % mix before the HDR gain).
- Glow that changes with the render scale / upscaler: the look at 1x and at 4K must match (levels are defined in screen fractions, not texels).
- HUD or menu text blurring. In SDR the HUD gets no glow at all; in HDR a soft low glow over the HUD is accepted for now (owner, option A).
- A visible pop when the checkbox or slider changes.

## Constraints from the current renderer (verified in the code)

- The game renders into an 8-bit EFB (RGBA8). With an upscaler the scene is reconstructed at output size at the scene boundary (`gx::SceneBoundary`), then the post chain and the HUD draw on top. HDR is an encode pass at present: `hdr.slang` decodes gamma 2.2, expands luminance above knee 0.6 from paper white to peak, converts to BT.2020 + PQ; overlays (port menu) go through a separate layer. Captures stay SDR.
- Pixels near 1.0 in the 8-bit buffer carry no information about how bright the source was. Both the SDR and the HDR look therefore need synthesised headroom (see Decisions).
- `VulkanRenderer::set_bloom(bool)` exists; the game's composite is skipped in `record_pass` (`renderer/vulkan_renderer.cpp`, `if (!bloom_ && gx::bloom_composite(draw)) continue;`). `Options::bloom` / `graphics.bloom` / `--no-bloom` exist and are exposed as a "disable" checkbox in both UIs (checked = no glow).
- Options plumbing for a new setting: `game/options.h`, `options.cpp`, `options_config.cpp`, `live_settings.{h,cpp}`, `settings_menu.cpp`, `launcher/main.cpp`, `launcher/translations.inc` (13 languages), tests `test_options*`, `test_live_settings`, `test_settings_menu`. Effects live in `renderer/` as passes recorded on the render thread; nothing touches guest threads; no pixel readback.
- Frame generation takes a HUD-less copy of the frame before the HUD packet (`docs/upscaling.md`); in HDR the plan routes that copy through the same encoder.

## Decisions made with the owner

- Variant 5 look (above). Replaces the game's bloom entirely.
- Checkbox plus slider, slider 0..200 % default 100 %, 0 % behaves like off.
- HDR: glow may touch the HUD (option A); revisit with option B only if it looks bad.

## Decisions made by the designer (the owner has not seen these; they are calls, not agreements)

1. **Source of brightness.** Because the buffer is 8-bit, the bloom input is the scene colour passed through a fixed inverse tone expansion above a knee (the same shape as `hdr_expand`, with a fixed headroom ratio in SDR and the user's peak/paper ratio in HDR). A pixel at 1.0 then counts as several times white, so lamps glow harder than white walls.
2. **Where it is computed.** At the scene boundary, before the HUD is drawn, in an RGBA16F mip pyramid derived from the (upscaled) scene colour. Pass structure: Karis-weighted 13-tap downsample for level 0, plain 13-tap downsamples after, tent upsample accumulating back up, six levels, levels sized by fractions of the output (first level is half resolution, so cost is independent of render scale beyond the first read).
3. **Where it is composited.**
   - SDR: added to the scene colour at the boundary (in linear light, then back to the 8-bit EFB), so the HUD drawn afterwards is untouched.
   - HDR: kept as a float texture and added in the HDR encode pass in nits (so it can exceed paper white, bounded by peak). Because the HUD is already baked into the slot image by then, the glow is added over the HUD too. **Owner decision (option A): accepted for the first version.** If it looks bad, the fallback is option B: encode the scene and the HUD in separate layers so the glow stays off the HUD (a larger change to the HDR pass); do not build B now.
4. **Strength.** Slider percent multiplies a base mix of 0.15 in SDR and 0.15 x 2.5 in HDR (the values used in the comparison). These constants are tuned in the game, not final.
5. **Settings.** `graphics.bloom` stays the on/off key (true = new bloom; the stored meaning does not flip, the UI checkbox becomes positive "Bloom" instead of "Disable glow"). New key `graphics.bloom_strength`, integer 0..200, default 100; out-of-range values are refused like the other config values. CLI: `--no-bloom` unchanged, new `--bloom-strength N`.
6. **Unsupported GPUs.** Needs only compute and RGBA16F storage images (universal on supported devices); no feature gating beyond the existing scene-target path. When the scene-target path is not active (effects off, upscaler off) the bloom still runs by making the targets exist, as the other effects do.

## Starting constants (designer's calls, tuned by eye in the game; acceptance is the owner's eye, not a number)

From the comparison script (variant 5): level weights `[0.20, 0.20, 0.18, 0.16, 0.14, 0.12]` for levels 1..6 (level 0 is the Karis-filtered half-resolution base); each level softened with two passes of the `[0.25, 0.5, 0.25]` kernel (the 13-tap downsample plus tent upsample replaces this in the real pass); the result is **added** to the scene (scaled, not lerped): `scene + bloom * 0.15 * strength` in SDR, `* 0.15 * 2.5 * strength` in HDR (in nits space, before the peak clamp). No threshold. Level `i` is output (scene target) size divided by `2^(i+1)`, so the look follows the screen, not the texel count.
Not in the script (the script worked on a float scene): the inverse expansion. Starting values: knee 0.6 (as `kHdrKnee`), SDR headroom ratio 4 (a pixel at 1.0 counts as 4x white), HDR headroom = peak / paper white. Expose these as constants in the math header so they can be tuned with an A/B step early in the plan.

## More calls by the designer (answers to the builder check)

- No scene boundary in a frame (menus, movies, black frames): no bloom that frame. Without an upscaler the pyramid starts from the internal-size scene target; levels are fractions of it, so the look matches across render scales.
- The SDR composite decodes with gamma 2.2 (same as `hdr.slang`, not the piecewise sRGB curve), adds, re-encodes, and applies a static ordered-dither of +-0.5 LSB to avoid banding in the dark gradients. The dither is a fixed pattern, so deterministic runs stay identical.
- Frame generation: the bloom is part of the HUD-less copy in both SDR and HDR, so real and generated frames match.
- Config: `graphics.bloom=false` (the old "disable glow") now means bloom off; no migration, no default reset.

## Left to the builder

Pass and shader structure, push-constant packing, exact tap weights, pyramid sizes at odd resolutions, the cost budget method (use the pass timers and `--profile` as the other effects do; target at most 0.4 ms at 1440p on the RTX 4080), test names, the translation strings (13 languages), how the launcher lays out checkbox and slider, how frame generation's HUD-less copy gets the bloom in HDR.

## Testing expectations

- Host tests: pure math header (inverse expansion, strength mapping, level sizes), options/config round trip, live settings, menu.
- GPU smoke section (`renderer_effects_smoke --bloom`): a dark frame with one bright square gives a glow that decays with distance, symmetric, and zero beyond the last level; strength 0 equals off exactly; checkbox toggled 20 times creates no per-frame resources; `--validation` clean; SDR and HDR fixtures.
- Live: one harness run on the first train scene with bloom on and off, captures compared by eye; HDR checked by the owner on the monitor (captures stay SDR).
