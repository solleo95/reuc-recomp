## Native ultrawide - owner decision 2026-10-28

A player on DirectX 12 at about 21:9 saw the whole picture (scene, interface, fonts) stretched. The owner chose a native ultrawide with no bars and no cropping on Vulkan, DirectX 11 and DirectX 12.

The renderer stretches the EFB over the window, so the game is asked for the wide picture itself. `game/intro.cpp` wraps `C_MTXPerspective` (80265464, `entry 80265468` in `project/uc.cfg`, `@80265464 MTXPerspective` in `project/hle_map.txt`): on a wider window the aspect argument grows by 1 / k, k = 16:9 / window aspect (a taller window shrinks the vertical tangent instead; `renderer/wide_view.h` holds the factors, set from the window size and updated on every resize). Every projection the game builds, and everything it derives from it, sees the wide picture: the 3D scene, the pointer's range (the game clamps its pixel position to 1..639 x 1..479 in its own space, now the whole window), the hit tests and the culling, and everything it lays over the scene at a projected point (pickup icons, glints, markers). At 16:9 or with `--no-ultrawide` the original function runs unchanged (the arguments are not even rewritten). The guest's state now depends on the window shape, so in a run that must be the same everywhere (network play, deterministic runs, demo record/play) the factors stay 1 and the picture is stretched as before; peers with different window shapes would otherwise desync at the first state hash. The aim stays plain: `aim = ndc * (0.8, 0.6)` at any shape, the mouse extent is the window.

`renderer/ultrawide.h` (applied in `Packets::seal_batch`, so one code path serves all three backends) then keeps the 2D elements from being stretched: an orthographic draw is narrowed by k about the screen centre in a frame without a 3D scene (menus), and about the centre of its cluster in a frame with a 3D scene (clusters are draws whose rectangles touch: the glyphs of a line; a panel wider or taller than 0.9 NDC never bridges two clusters), so every element keeps its place and loses only the stretch. A player's block is found by its HUD plate (the panel behind the name, bar, weapon and counts: a 220x64 texture): everything whose centre lies on it belongs to it, in whatever packet it is drawn (the ammo count comes apart from the bar), and its anchor comes from the plate alone. (Callsite 0x803B7FE4 is the game's font, it draws every text of every screen: using it as a bar marker made each text a "player block" and shifted pickup labels, the reload message and tutorials.) The pointer's draws (ring, dot, halo, wake) narrow about the reticle's centre. It follows `graphics.hud_position` / `--hud-position auto|center|edges`: with `edges` it keeps its margin from the screen edge, otherwise its centre moves in by k (the 16:9 place in the middle); `auto` (default) is edges up to about 21:9, the middle beyond. The combo is in the launcher's display page and in the in-game menu's main tab, applied live (`SettingsTargets::hud_position`).

- Left stretched: orthographic draws across the whole scaled axis (menu backgrounds, fades, flashes, cinematic bars) and the game's own passes over scratch areas of the EFB (every packet that ends in a copy as wide as the picture: the bloom chain, composites). Their glow-baked pictures (the title logo's glow is part of the full-width picture) therefore stay stretched against the narrowed logo.
- Full-screen pictures (an orthographic quad across the width with a 640x480 texture): movies keep 16:9 (the movie player's callsite 0x801619C4), still screens (Nintendo, CAPCOM) 4:3, both clipped by a scissor to their own area (the game's quad is 10% wider than the screen).
- Button prompts (pictures over the SDK quads of the text) are narrowed when the packet is sealed, with the anchor of the text line they touch (the line came in an earlier packet of the frame, kept in `WideFrame`), or about the screen centre in a menu; their clip rectangle moves with them. Late aim and the pointer use plain aim units.
- `graphics.ultrawide` / `--no-ultrawide`: off stretches the 16:9 picture over the window as before.

Findings of the investigation (2026-10-28): the pointer's range is the game's, not the host's: the pad manager at 8043A82C passes the raw `KPAD` position (-1..1) and a virtual method called from 8003D1AC turns it into pixels `320 + 400 * aim` and clamps it (the unclamped value is only in a local). The camera object at 8044B4E0 holds the view (3x4) and the projection (4x4) the game passes to `GXSetProjection`. Tools: `UC_READ_TRAP=1` with `UC_WRITE_TRAP` logs reads as well as writes of watched guest words (game/write_trap.cpp); `UC_PACKET_DUMP` lines show the projection kind, the ortho x range, the maps and stage orders of a draw.

Known limits: every C_MTXPerspective is widened, including a render-to-texture camera (not seen in the scenes looked at); cameras built another way stay 16:9; the game culls by its own tests, so far to the sides something may appear late (not seen in the train car or chapter 2 at 21:9 and 32:9); no launcher or in-game switch for `graphics.ultrawide`; a taller window (4:3, 16:10) is covered by the same code but was only looked at in the train car.

## Reticle look (menu tab "Reticle")

`graphics.reticle_*` in config.json, live from the in-game menu; `renderer/reticle_style.h` holds the style, `late_aim.h apply_reticle_style` applies it to the flagged reticle draws of every packet (after `mark_reticle_draws`), so all three backends share it. Parts (found with draw hiding in a spike): the green ammo arc is the 154 texture of callsite 80393B0C, the player's circle a 120 texture with ink on its diagonals (178 is its halo), the four white marks are other, untinted 120 textures that blink, the laser dot is callsite 8006D174, the wake 8006CED4. Hide drops the draws; colour keeps each vertex's brightness and swaps the hue; the dot is scaled about its centroid; the partner (a draw that is not late-aimed while a second pad exists) is faded through the vertex alpha; the wake's length is `set_wake_seconds` times the option (4 steps of 30 Hz in single-player). Texture-replaced (BC7) circles fall back to the tint to tell circle from marks.
